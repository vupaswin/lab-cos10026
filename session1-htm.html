4 Different Types of Fonts

Fonts give your website personality. Here are the 4 main types used in web design:

1. Sans-Serif (Modern & Clean)

"Inter" — No little lines on letter edges. Easy to read on phones and laptops.
font-family: 'Inter', sans-serif;

2. Serif (Classic & Formal)

"Playfair Display" — Has small feet/strokes at letter tips. Looks like books and newspapers.
font-family: 'Playfair Display', serif;

3. Monospace (Coding Font)

"Fira Code" — Every letter has the exact same width. Used by programmers for code.
font-family: 'Fira Code', monospace;

4. Handwriting (Friendly & Fun)

"Caveat" — Looks like real hand writing. Good for notes, logos, and signatures.
font-family: 'Caveat', cursive;

1. The Box Model (How Elements Are Sized)

Think of every HTML element as a gift box:

1. Margin (Orange) — Empty space outside the box to push other boxes away.
2. Border (Yellow) — The line or wall around your box.
3. Padding (Green) — Space inside the box so text doesn't touch the wall.
4. Content (Blue) — The actual text or photo inside!

Simple Rule: Always use box-sizing: border-box; in CSS. That way, if you say width: 300px;, it stays 300px even if you add padding or borders!

6. CSS Measurement Units (px, rem, em)

Unit 1: rem (Best for Text)

1rem is normally 16px. If a user increases text size in their browser settings, everything in rem grows nicely.

Unit 2: em (Best for Buttons)

Unit 3: ch (Comfortable Reading Width)

This text uses max-width: 65ch;. It means lines never become longer than ~65 letters, so your eyes don't get tired reading across wide screens.
